About this research

The question this page explores: do the new AI weather ensembles add useful early warning of the large-scale patterns that drive large fire growth in California, compared with the traditional physics-based ensembles?

What's shown

  • Four openly available global ensembles, each cut to the eastern Pacific and western U.S.: two AI (ECMWF AIFS ENS, NOAA AIGEFS) and two physics-based (ECMWF IFS ENS, NOAA GEFS). Each model's newest complete run is used, updated once a day.
  • "Northern California" and "Southern California" are the two California Geographic Area Coordination Center areas (codes ONCC and OSCC). They're used only as fixed regions for the statistics.

How the "calibrated" numbers were made

  • History, 2001–present: days when a region's large fires grew by at least 5,000 acres in total, dated from NASA FIRMS satellite fire detections inside mapped fire perimeters.
  • For each weather measure (surface pressure gradients from airport observations, upper-air soundings, reanalysis temperatures and 500 hPa patterns), we found the cutoff beyond which those days were at least twice as common as usual for that time of year ("elevated"), or four times ("high"). The offshore-wind seasons (Northern California Sep–Nov, Southern California Oct–Jan) are scored on their own.
  • The forecasts are then scored against those cutoffs as the % of ensemble members beyond them.
  • Thunderstorm rows (May–Oct) are fit to 25 years of cloud-to-ground lightning strikes instead: a CAPE (instability) measure over each area, its cutoff set where days with ≥100 strikes are twice as common as usual, and the odds of a new large lightning fire from the fire records. Only the two models that forecast CAPE (ECMWF IFS ENS, NOAA GEFS) feed them.

Limitations

  • The cutoffs come from station observations, not from each model's own history. A model's bias at a station isn't corrected yet.
  • "Times usual odds" describes history. It isn't a fire forecast: ignitions, fuels and suppression aren't in it. Summer weather patterns alone are only weakly related to large growth (lightning and fuels dominate); the offshore-wind seasons are where the signal is strongest.
  • AI ensembles are new and their behavior in extreme events is still being studied.
