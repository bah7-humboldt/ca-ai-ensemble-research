Reading the regimes

The "500 hPa regimes" section of the main page.

What a regime is

Every fire-season day from 2001–2025 was sorted, by its 500 hPa (~18,000 ft) height anomaly pattern over the eastern Pacific and western U.S., into one of 6 groups that recur again and again (k-means clustering). Each thumbnail is the average pattern of its group. Red = higher than normal heights (ridge), blue = lower (trough). Click a thumbnail's letter for its page: how often it happens by month, and the fire growth that came with it.

Each regime is labeled with its historical odds of a ≥5,000-acre growth day in Northern and Southern California, compared with the usual odds for the time of year (×1 = usual).

The probability grid

Each member's daily-mean pattern is matched to the closest regime. A box is the share of all members (models weighted equally) in that regime on that day; hover it for each model. A column dominated by one dark box means the models agree on the pattern. Spread-out columns mean several patterns are possible, which is common past about day 7.

The odds line

For each day, the regime probabilities times each regime's historical odds, added up: a pattern-only estimate of how the chance of a big growth day compares with usual. Values near 1 are ordinary; 1.3–1.5 is a notably fire-friendly pattern by this measure. It's a modest, broad-brush signal; the calibrated outlook rows capture the sharper wind and heat signals.

Regimes and pyroconvection

In seven studied California fires (262 fire days, 9 pyrocumulonimbus days), pyroCb days by regime: A 3, B 0, C 4, D 2, E 0, F 0. Every pyroCb day was in A, C or D, and none came under B, E or F. The average map on the pyroCb days has an upper ridge over British Columbia with a trough offshore, which turns mid-level flow southerly and brings moisture north under the heat. Together with 700 hPa moisture at the fire of ~3.7 g/kg or more (a field the models on this page forecast), A/C/D marked all 9 pyroCb days and 43/186 of the days without deep plume cloud; fire by fire, 6/9 caught and 49/224 flagged. Regime C is also the offshore-wind regime, and its dry days made the biggest runs with no deep plume cloud at all, so the regime alone is a broad filter; the moisture is what separates the two. Each regime's page lists its pyroCb days; details on Pyroconvection under the scenarios.
