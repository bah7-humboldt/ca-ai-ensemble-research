The grid of colored boxes on the main page.

What each row isONCCOSCCHI

Each row is one weather measure (a surface pressure gradient, an upper-air wind or temperature, or a region-wide temperature anomaly) with a cutoff learned from history: on days past that cutoff, California fire days with at least 5,000 acres of growth were about twice as likely as usual for the time of year (the "elevated" cutoff). Some rows also have a "high" cutoff, about four times as likely. Click a row's About link for its stations, history and the fires behind it.

Rows are grouped by region and season: offshore season (Northern California Sep–Nov, Southern California Oct–Jan) or the general fire season. Only rows with real skill (AUC at least 0.65) are shown, so the rows change with the season.

Each box is the share of all ensemble members (the four models weighted equally) past the cutoff on that fire day. Hover a box for each model's share.

  • A run of dark boxes several days out, in several rows at once, is the strongest signal: many members, several measures.
  • Light boxes don't mean "no fire weather". The measure isn't expected past its cutoff, and other drivers (lightning, fuels) can still matter.
  • A fire day runs 6 AM to 6 AM Pacific, matching how the satellite growth days are counted.

The two views: members and weather + fuels oddsONCCOSCCHI

% of members past the cutoff is the weather alone. Weather + fuels odds adds the fuels: for each day it takes the FEMS 7-day NFDRS forecast (ERC, averaged over the row's footprint PSAs) and asks whether fuels will be critically dry (ERC at or above the 90th percentile of 2005–2025). Members past the cutoff then carry history's odds for "past cutoff on dry (or not dry) fuels", and the rest carry the odds for "not past". The box shows the result as times the usual odds of a ≥5,000-acre growth day.

  • History says weather and fuels multiply. Northern California warmth aloft on critically dry fuels has run about 8× the usual odds, and the same warmth on fuels below the 90th percentile about 0.9×.
  • Faded boxes are past the 7-day FEMS forecast; the last forecast day's fuels are carried forward.
  • Thin history is pulled toward the weather-only odds, so a combination seen on only a few dozen days can't swing the result.

The measuresONCCOSCCHI

  • Pressure gradients (SFO–WMC, LAX–TPH, LAX–DAG...): offshore wind.
  • Minimum relative humidity at inland airports (Sacramento, Redding, Santa Rosa, Burbank, Ontario, Paso Robles): surface dryness. Some of the strongest single measures in the record, e.g. Paso Robles and Ontario in the Santa Ana season.
  • Upper-air warmth and dryness from weather balloons (850 and 700 hPa) and region-wide reanalysis temperatures.
  • Each is scored only against fire growth inside its own footprint PSAs, and each page also shows its record for new large fires: days a fire that went on to burn 1,000+ acres started.

Thunderstorm rows (May–Oct)ONCCOSCC

The rows under Thunderstorms and new lightning fires work differently. Their measure is the number of lightning strikes the lightning formula expects across a footprint (the shape of ECMWF's own lightning scheme, Lopez 2016, on convective rain, cloud ice and water, CAPE and cloud base), and their cutoff was fit to observed lightning strikes: past it, days with at least 100 strikes in the footprint were much more common than usual for the date. Lightning starts more large fires than it grows in the week after, so the weather + fuels view for these rows shows the odds of a new large (1,000+ acre) lightning fire found that day or the next.

  • Only ECMWF IFS ENS carries the convective fields the formula needs, so only it feeds these rows, out to 14 fire days. Days 8–14 are shown faded: thunderstorm forecasts that far out have little skill, and they haven't been verified.
  • History: in Northern California, a day with 500+ strikes on critically dry fuels has run several times to over twenty times the usual odds of a new large lightning fire, depending on the area; on moister fuels much less.
  • The lightning history is licensed (Vaisala NLDN) and is only shown as derived counts, rates and maps.

The detail chart and the cutoff sliderONCCOSCCHI

Below the grid, the chart shows each model's share by day for the selected row. Drag the cutoff to see how the forecast changes with a stricter or looser threshold. The buttons jump back to the calibrated or "high" cutoff. A model that stays high at the "high" cutoff is showing a strong event, not just a marginal one.

CaveatsONCCOSCCHI

  • "× usual odds" describes history. It isn't a fire forecast: ignitions, fuels and suppression aren't in it.
  • The cutoffs come from station observations. Each model's own bias at a station isn't removed yet.
  • Each member counts once, and each model gets equal weight however many members it has.
