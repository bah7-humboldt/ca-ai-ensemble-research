The two stacked charts in "Offshore gradients and 850 hPa wind".

Top chart: the plumeONCCOSCC

Each colored line is one model's median member; the shaded band around it holds the middle 80% of its members (10th–90th percentile). Solid lines are AI models, dashed are physics models. Times are Pacific, every 12 hours.

  • Pressure gradients (hPa) are coast-minus-inland sea-level pressure. Negative = higher pressure inland = offshore wind. The more negative, the stronger the offshore push: SFO–WMC for Diablo/North wind events, LAX–DAG and LAX–TPH for Santa Anas, RDD–MFR for north wind down the Sacramento Valley, SFO–SAC for the Bay Area–Valley gradient.
  • 850 hPa offshore wind (kt) is the wind about 5,000 ft up at a point, counted only when it blows from the northeast-to-east; otherwise it's 0.
  • A narrow band means the members agree; a wide band means uncertainty. A band that dips well past the dashed line while the median doesn't means a minority of members carry a strong event.

The share of each model's members past the cutoff at each time. Drag the cutoff slider to try your own threshold. The starting values here are rule-of-thumb reference values; the calibrated outlook above uses cutoffs learned from history, measured as the day's strongest (most offshore) value.
