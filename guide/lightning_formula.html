Lightning formula (Lopez 2016, IFS ENS): how it's made and how well it doesONCCOSCCMethod 2 · ECMWF formula

The "Lightning formula" fields in the pattern map's field menu. Experimental.

What it showsONCCOSCC

For each 0.5° cell (about 35 miles across) over California and each fire day, the chance of at least one cloud-to-ground lightning strike, or of ten or more: the same cells and targets as the "Lightning potential" fields, so the two can be compared directly. It comes from ECMWF IFS ENS alone (50 members, 14 fire days), so it's the same map whichever model is picked. Past day 7 read it as a rough signal at most: thunderstorm forecasts that far out have little skill, and the formula's skill was only tested on the reanalysis, not on forecasts at any lead.

Why it existsONCCOSCC

ECMWF's forecast model computes lightning itself, with a scheme by Philippe Lopez (2016) that ties flash rates to the storms its convection scheme builds. Those lightning fields aren't in ECMWF's free open data, the public charts are images only, and the ERA5 reanalysis on the Copernicus Climate Data Store doesn't carry them either. Since 20 October 2026 the open data does carry the ingredients the scheme rests on, so this layer rebuilds the scheme's shape from them.

How it's madeONCCOSCC

Lopez's scheme: flash density = α · QR · √CAPE · min(zbase, 1.8 km)², where QR is a charging term (graupel colliding with cloud water and snow between the 0 °C and −25 °C levels) and zbase the convective cloud base. The open data has no graupel or cloud-base fields, so stand-ins are used:

  • Graupel → the convective precipitation rate (mm/h).
  • Cloud water and snow in the charging zone → column cloud ice + 0.7 × column cloud liquid (0.7 is Lopez's weight over land).
  • CAPE → most-unstable CAPE.
  • Cloud base → the lifting condensation level of a surface parcel (125 m per °C of 2 m dewpoint depression), capped at 1.8 km as in Lopez.

Lopez's exponents (1, 1, ½, 2) were tuned for his graupel and cloud-base fields, not these stand-ins, so the exponents were refit: convective rain0.125 · cloud ice/water1 · CAPE0.5 · cloud base4. (The best values for a_cp, b_q, c_cape, d_zb sat at the edge of the range searched.) The plain Lopez shape is scored below too.

  • Strikes: Vaisala NLDN cloud-to-ground strikes per cell per fire day (6 a.m.–6 a.m. Pacific), the same counts as the Lightning potential layer. The output is calibrated to these, so it means cloud-to-ground strikes, not ECMWF's total (in-cloud + ground) flashes.
  • Weather: ERA5 reanalysis versions of the same six fields, every 3 hours, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep.
  • Fitting: 2001–2020: the exponents by search (best ranking of strike days), a scale so expected strikes match observed, and curves turning a cell's expected strikes into a chance of ≥1 and ≥10. Testing: 2021–2025, years not used in fitting.
  • Forecasts: each IFS ENS member's fields every 6 hours, the formula per 6-hour window, summed to fire days (windows 12–12 UTC, within an hour of the fire day), then the members' chances averaged.

Assumptions and limitsONCCOSCC

  • Summer only so far. It was fit and tested on Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep. In other months the curves are carried over untested; read cool-season values with care (spring and fall storms are colder-based and lower-topped).
  • Trained on the reanalysis, run on forecasts. ERA5 is an older, coarser version of the same model. Today's IFS makes more and sharper convective rain, which can push the chances higher than the history behind them. Live forecasts can only be scored from 20 October 2026 on, when the ingredients entered the open data.
  • Strike totals drift. Expected strikes came to 0.8× the observed total in 2001–2010 and 2.0× in 2016–2025. The NLDN network was upgraded around 2013, which changed how weak strokes are classed, and the reanalysis's convection changed too. Rankings and chances held up across the record (the AUC by year above), but treat the formula's strike counts as relative, not literal.
  • Stand-ins for the physics. Convective rain stands in for graupel, and column totals for the charging layer; a deep column of cloud water below the freezing level counts the same as one up in the charging zone.
  • Daily, not hourly. The reanalysis is sampled every 3 hours and the forecasts every 6, so short-lived storms between samples are averaged in, not caught as peaks.
  • Land only. Fit on California's land cells; nothing is shown offshore.
  • Not ECMWF's product. A rebuild of the scheme's shape, calibrated here. ECMWF's own lightning forecast is on its public charts.
  • Licensed data. The strike history is Vaisala NLDN, used under license; only derived probabilities and maps are shown.
