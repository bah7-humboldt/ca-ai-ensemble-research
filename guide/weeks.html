Reading the week 1 and week 2 patterns

The "Week 1 and week 2 patterns" section of the main page.

The windows

Week 1 is days 3–7 and week 2 is days 8–14, counting today as day 1. Each member's 500 hPa (~18,000 ft) height is averaged over the whole window. Averaging over a week filters out the day-to-day timing differences that make single days past about day 7 so uncertain, and leaves the broad pattern: where the ridges and troughs tend to sit.

The probability map

0102033–678090100%

The share of all members whose week-average 500 hPa height is above normal for those dates, with the four models weighted equally.

  • Dark red: nearly every member, in every model, has a ridge there. Expect warmer and drier than normal, with sinking air and poor overnight humidity recovery.
  • Dark blue: nearly every member has a trough. Cooler, more onshore flow, better chances of precipitation.
  • White (33–67%): the members are split. The week could go either way there.
  • Heights have trended upward with the warming climate, so above-normal is somewhat more common than 50% even in an ordinary pattern. Look for the deep colors.

The scenario cards

Every member's week-average pattern from all four models is pooled and sorted into the 4 most distinct groups (k-means clustering on the main pattern shapes). Each card is one group:

  • The map is the group's average 500 hPa anomaly (red = ridge, blue = trough) with its average heights as contours (dam).
  • "% of all members" is the group's share with each model weighted equally. The cards are ordered from most to least likely.
  • The bars show how much of each model's own ensemble falls in that group. Solid bars are AI models, hatched bars are physics models. When one scenario holds most of every model's members, the week is fairly confident. When different models favor different scenarios, the forecast hinges on which model is right. That's worth watching as the week gets closer.

These are this run's scenarios, so the letters mean different patterns each day. The 500 hPa regimes further down the page are fixed patterns from history, with their fire records.
