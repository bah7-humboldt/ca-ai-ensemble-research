Pyroconvection under the weather scenariosONCCOSCC

Which days 23 large California fires made pyrocumulonimbus (pyroCb) or pyrocumulus (pyroCu), and which of this page's 500 hPa regimes and documented patterns they fell in. Research, not a forecast product.

What was studiedONCCOSCC

  • Dixie 2021, Carr 2018, Loyalton 2020, North Complex (incl. Bear) 2020, August Complex 2020, Creek 2020, Caldor 2021, Ranch (Mendocino Complex) 2018, Camp 2018, Hennessey (LNU Complex) 2020, Sugar (Beckwourth Complex) 2021, Antelope 2021, McKinney 2022, Mosquito 2022, Park 2024, Ferguson 2018, Woolsey 2018, Castle (SQF Complex) 2020, Bobcat 2020, York 2023, Borel 2024, Bridge 2024, Line 2024: 491 fire days (13 pyroCb, 54 pyroCu, 76 with cold cloud over the fire from ordinary storms, 348 without deep plume cloud).
  • PyroCb = cloud tops colder than −40 °C rooted on the fire in GOES-17/16 infrared (10-minute), reviewed by eye; pyroCu = fire-rooted tops between −20 and −40 °C. Usable NEXRAD radars confirm them: the plume's echo column reaches ≥ ~11 km on every pyroCb day.
  • Atmosphere: the pyroCb firepower threshold (PFT) of Tory and Kepert (2021, Weather and Forecasting 36, 439–456) from hourly HRRR columns over each day's most active front. It is the least fire heat output the air needs for a pyroCb.

FindingsONCCOSCC

  • PyroCb days fell mostly in regimes C, D and A (12 of 13), once in regime B (Castle, 23 Aug 2020) and never in E or F. Their average 500 hPa map has a strong ridge over British Columbia with a trough offshore in the northeast Pacific: southerly flow that carries mid-level (monsoon) moisture north under a hot ridge.
  • The biggest growth days without deep plume cloud have the opposite map: a ridge over California and a trough over the northern Rockies, dry northerly or offshore flow. 41 of the 49 northeast-foehn days in the study made no deep plume cloud, and the two biggest days of all (8 Sep 2020, North Complex/Bear and August Complex) were offshore-wind runs with no pyroCb potential.
  • 49% of all growth came on days when the air was too dry for a pyroCb at any hour (no PFT solution); 6% came on pyroCb days.
  • The best single measure is the fire's power against the air's threshold (FP/PFT, same hour): at about 0.24 or more it caught 11 of 13 pyroCb days and flagged 44 of 402 other days when each fire was held out. The cut fitted on the first seven fires, tried unchanged on 16 more, caught 3 of 4 new pyroCb days and flagged 11 of 162 new no-plume days. It works in the north (7 of 8 pyroCb days, 12 of 220 no-plume days flagged) and the south (5 of 5, 8 of 128).
  • Two ways to a pyroCb: a modest fire under very favourable air (Dixie, Loyalton, Castle, Line; thresholds 6-57 GW) or a big fire under marginal air (Creek, Carr, Sugar; 110-220 GW of fire power against 176-384 GW thresholds; Creek on 6 Sep, with a 25 GW threshold, was the exception).
  • LANDFIRE fuels (the newest vintage before each fire, with later disturbance masked) set the energy scale but do not separate pyroCb days: fuel type, load, canopy and slope are nearly the same on pyroCb, pyroCu and no-plume days.
  • Forecastable: in a morning hindcast (05 PDT HRRR, yesterday’s fire front), no pyroCb formed on any of the 236 days the forecast said the air could not support one. The forecast threshold (PFT 360 GW or less) flagged all 12 pyroCb days that had a morning forecast, along with 32 of 52 pyroCu days and 85 of 329 days with no deep plume cloud; adding yesterday’s satellite fire power cut the no-plume flags to 43 while catching 10 of 12.

Regime plus 700 hPa moisture: a pattern-level signalONCCOSCC

The 500 hPa regime and the 700 hPa moisture at the fire (ERA5, the moister of 11 a.m. and 5 p.m.) are both fields the models on this page forecast days ahead. Together they cut the regime's false alarms by about two thirds; they're still weaker than the same-day fire-power test, so read them as "the pattern allows pyroCb", not a forecast of one.

CaveatsONCCOSCC

  • 23 fires and 13 pyroCb days: the regime counts and thresholds are indicative, not calibrated forecasts.
  • Pattern rules are the Northern California ones from the Documented patterns page; 8 of the 23 fires are in Southern California.
  • Days with ordinary thunderstorms over or beside the fire (76) are left out: satellite cloud tops can't separate a pyroCb inside them.
  • Some dramatic plumes stayed warmer than -40 C at the GOES cloud top (Park 25 Jul 2024, Bridge 10 Sep 2024), so they count as pyroCu or no deep plume here.
  • The record Creek pyroCb (5 Sep 2020) formed in hours when HRRR gave no pyroCb solution; a 'not possible' from the model doesn't rule out a pyroCb from a fire putting out ~150 GW.
  • The McKinney pyroCb (29 Jul 2022) formed around 23 PDT on the fire's first day, after the 12-22 PDT window the study scores, so FP/PFT missed it.
  • GOES-17's archive has no data between about 11 p.m. and 4 a.m. on many 2020-21 nights, so late-night pyroCbs can be missed.
