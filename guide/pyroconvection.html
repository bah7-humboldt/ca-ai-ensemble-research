Pyroconvection under the weather scenarios

Which days seven large California fires made pyrocumulonimbus (pyroCb) or pyrocumulus (pyroCu), and which of this page's 500 hPa regimes and documented patterns they fell in. Research, not a forecast product.

What was studied

  • Dixie 2021, Carr 2018, Loyalton 2020, North Complex (incl. Bear) 2020, August Complex 2020, Creek 2020, Caldor 2021: 262 fire days (9 pyroCb, 38 pyroCu, 29 with cold cloud over the fire from ordinary storms, 186 without deep plume cloud).
  • PyroCb = cloud tops colder than −40 °C rooted on the fire in GOES-17/16 infrared (10-minute), reviewed by eye; pyroCu = fire-rooted tops between −20 and −40 °C. Usable NEXRAD radars confirm them: the plume's echo column reaches ≥ ~11 km on every pyroCb day.
  • Atmosphere: the pyroCb firepower threshold (PFT) of Tory and Kepert (2021, Weather and Forecasting 36, 439–456) from hourly HRRR columns over each day's most active front. It is the least fire heat output the air needs for a pyroCb.

Findings

  • PyroCb days fell only in regimes C, A and D, never in B, E or F. Their average 500 hPa map has a strong ridge over British Columbia with a trough offshore in the northeast Pacific: southerly flow that carries mid-level (monsoon) moisture north under a hot ridge.
  • The biggest growth days without deep plume cloud have the opposite map: a ridge at the California coast and a trough over the northern Rockies, dry northerly or offshore flow. 21 of the 27 northeast-foehn days in the study made no deep plume cloud, and the two biggest days of all seven fires (8 Sep 2020, North Complex/Bear and August Complex) were offshore-wind runs with no pyroCb potential.
  • 57% of all growth came on days when the air was too dry for a pyroCb at any hour (no PFT solution); 9% came on pyroCb days.
  • The best single measure is the fire's power against the air's threshold (FP/PFT, same hour): at about 0.25 or more it caught 8 of 9 pyroCb days and flagged 22 of 224 other days when each fire was held out. Moisture, wind, plume height or fire power alone all did worse.
  • Two ways to a pyroCb: a modest fire under very favourable air (Dixie, Loyalton; threshold 16-57 GW) or a huge fire under marginal air (Creek, Carr; 160-220 GW of fire power against a 190-380 GW threshold).
  • LANDFIRE fuels (pre-fire LF 2016, with 2017-2020 disturbance masked) set the energy scale but do not separate pyroCb days: fuel type, load, canopy and slope are nearly the same on pyroCb, pyroCu and no-plume days. A fuel-based fire power (area x fuel load) agrees with GOES on most pyroCb days.
  • Forecastable: in a morning hindcast (05 PDT HRRR, yesterday’s fire front), no pyroCb formed on any of the 152 days the forecast said the air could not support one, and the forecast threshold flagged all 9 pyroCb days (along with 22 of 38 pyroCu days and 37 of 186 days with no deep plume cloud). Adding yesterday’s satellite fire power cut the no-plume false alarms to 19 while still catching 7 of 9. PFT computed on the AI ensembles’ standard pressure levels matched the full column.

Regime plus 700 hPa moisture: a pattern-level signal

The 500 hPa regime and the 700 hPa moisture at the fire (ERA5, the moister of 11 a.m. and 5 p.m.) are both fields the models on this page forecast days ahead. Together they cut the regime's false alarms by about two thirds; they're still weaker than the same-day fire-power test, so read them as "the pattern allows pyroCb", not a forecast of one.

Caveats

  • Seven fires and nine pyroCb days: the regime counts and thresholds are indicative, not calibrated forecasts.
  • Pattern rules are the Northern California ones from the Documented patterns page; Creek 2020 is a Southern Sierra fire.
  • Days with ordinary thunderstorms over or beside the fire (29) are left out: satellite cloud tops can't separate a pyroCb inside them.
  • The record Creek pyroCb (5 Sep 2020) formed in hours when HRRR gave no pyroCb solution; a 'not possible' from the model doesn't rule out a pyroCb from a fire putting out ~150 GW.
  • GOES-17's archive has no data between about 11 p.m. and 4 a.m. on many 2020-21 nights, so late-night pyroCbs can be missed.
