What it measuresOSCC

The lowest relative humidity of the fire day at Ontario International Airport, from the airport's hourly observations, taken at the 00, 06, 12 and 18 UTC reports (the late-afternoon 00 UTC report is usually the day's driest), so a model's 12-hourly forecast can be compared with it.

Why it matters for fireOSCC

Low afternoon humidity dries the fine fuels that carry fire, and a dry night with poor humidity recovery lets fires keep running overnight. The models' 2 m humidity at the nearest 0.5° cell is scored against it.

Stations

  • ONT: Ontario International Airport (34.06°N, 117.60°W)

Dot: the airport weather station.

Where fire grew: the satellite footprintOSCC

This measure is scored against fire growth inside its footprint: Central and South Coast and the Southern California mountains (Santa Ana), PSAs SC07 Central Coast, SC08 South Coast, SC09 Western Mountains, SC10 Eastern Mountains, SC11 Southern Mountains. Only acres that satellites saw burning inside those PSAs count toward its big growth days.

On a typical offshore season day, 1.5% of days have at least 5,000 acres of satellite-mapped fire growth in Central and South Coast and the Southern California mountains (Santa Ana). When this measure is past the elevated cutoff, such days are about 2.2 times as likely as usual for the same time of year. The cutoff is the most inclusive one that reaches ×2 with at least 25 days of history behind it.

Lift vs cutoff

How the odds grow as the cutoff gets stricter (lower values). Cutoffs beyond the "high" mark rest on fewer days, so that end of the curve is noisier.

Big growth days vs all days

The share of offshore season days at each value: all days (dashed) and days with ≥5,000 acres of growth (orange). The further the orange curve sits toward the cutoff side, the more useful the measure.

The biggest growth days and what this measure showedOSCC

Central and South Coast and the Southern California mountains (Santa Ana), offshore season, 2001–present. Acres are only those inside the footprint; fire names and acres from satellite detections inside mapped perimeters. 46 of 47 days with ≥5,000 acres were past the elevated cutoff ("no obs": a station didn't report that day).

The five biggest days

  • Sun Oct 26, 2003: ONT minimum relative humidity was 19.9 % (past the elevated cutoff). In SC08 South Coast, the CEDAR ran 20 miles and grew 152,345 acres that fire day (307,834 acres across the footprint).
  • Sun Oct 21, 2007: ONT minimum relative humidity was 5.2 % (past the high cutoff). With ERC at the 67th percentile (33) in SC08 South Coast, the WITCH ran 23 miles and grew 73,136 acres that fire day (185,542 acres across the footprint).
  • Mon Oct 22, 2007: ONT minimum relative humidity was 2.6 % (past the high cutoff). With ERC at the 82nd percentile (38) in SC08 South Coast, the WITCH ran 12 miles and grew 83,697 acres that fire day (169,796 acres across the footprint).
  • Sat Oct 25, 2003: ONT minimum relative humidity was 12.5 % (past the high cutoff). In SC08 South Coast, the SIMI ran 20 miles and grew 53,810 acres that fire day (147,290 acres across the footprint).
  • Mon Oct 27, 2003: ONT minimum relative humidity was 15.5 % (past the elevated cutoff). In SC11 Southern Mountains, the CEDAR ran 5 miles and grew 33,537 acres that fire day (96,524 acres across the footprint).

New large firesOSCC

Growth isn't the only question: offshore and dry days also start the fires that become large. On the 1,336 offshore season days past the elevated cutoff, a fire that went on to burn at least 1,000 acres started in Central and South Coast and the Southern California mountains (Santa Ana) on 50 of them (3.7% of those days, against 1.7% on a typical offshore season day); about ×2.2 the usual odds for the date. That's 96% of the 52 large-fire starts there since 2001.
