OAK 850 hPa temperature anomaly (morning)

Calibrated predictor · Northern California, offshore season (Sep, Oct, Nov) · footprint: Bay Area, North Bay and Sacramento Valley foothills (Diablo / North wind) · today's run 2026-10-17 10:25 PT

What it measures

Air temperature at about 5,000 ft (850 hPa) from the Oakland weather balloon, early morning (12 UTC, ~5 AM PDT), minus the normal for that date (2001–2025).

Why it matters for fire

Warm air aloft marks a ridge of high pressure: sinking, drying air, a lid on the marine layer, hot and dry days, and on offshore days a warm, dry wind. It's measured against the date's normal so a warm August day isn't credited just for being August.

Where fire grew: the satellite footprint

This measure is scored against fire growth inside its footprint: Bay Area, North Bay and Sacramento Valley foothills (Diablo / North wind), PSAs NC02 Mid Coast To Mendocino, NC03A Bay Marine, NC03B Diablo-Santa Cruz Mtns, NC05 Sac Valley/Foothills. Only acres that satellites saw burning inside those PSAs count toward its big growth days.

On a typical offshore season day, 2.0% of days have at least 5,000 acres of satellite-mapped fire growth in Bay Area, North Bay and Sacramento Valley foothills (Diablo / North wind). When this measure is past the elevated cutoff, such days are about 2.1 times as likely as usual for the same time of year. The cutoff is the most inclusive one that reaches ×2 with at least 25 days of history behind it.

Lift vs cutoff

How the odds grow as the cutoff gets stricter (higher values). Cutoffs beyond the "high" mark rest on fewer days, so that end of the curve is noisier.

The share of offshore season days at each value: all days (dashed) and days with ≥5,000 acres of growth (orange). The further the orange curve sits toward the cutoff side, the more useful the measure.

The biggest growth days and what this measure showed

Bay Area, North Bay and Sacramento Valley foothills (Diablo / North wind), offshore season, 2001–present. Acres are only those inside the footprint; fire names and acres from satellite detections inside mapped perimeters. 27 of 46 days with ≥5,000 acres were past the elevated cutoff ("no obs": a station didn't report that day).

The five biggest days

  • Sun Oct 8, 2017: OAK 850 hPa temperature anomaly was 5.0 °C (past the elevated cutoff). With ERC at the 80th percentile (45) in NC02 Mid Coast To Mendocino, the TUBBS (CENTRAL LNU COMPLEX) ran 13 miles and grew 23,836 acres that fire day (99,536 acres across the footprint).
  • Sun Sep 27, 2020: OAK 850 hPa temperature anomaly was 5.1 °C (past the elevated cutoff). With ERC at the 66th percentile (38) in NC02 Mid Coast To Mendocino, the GLASS ran 17 miles and grew 41,076 acres that fire day (93,954 acres across the footprint).
  • Tue Sep 8, 2020: OAK 850 hPa temperature anomaly was 9.1 °C (past the elevated cutoff). With ERC at the 99th percentile (58) in NC04 Northwestern Mtn, the AUGUST COMPLEX ran 17 miles and grew 197,534 acres that fire day (90,211 acres across the footprint).
  • Thu Nov 8, 2018: OAK 850 hPa temperature anomaly was -0.1 °C (not past the cutoff). With ERC at the 74th percentile (50) in NC05 Sac Valley/Foothills, the CAMP ran 27 miles and grew 102,838 acres that fire day (75,244 acres across the footprint).
  • Mon Oct 9, 2017: OAK 850 hPa temperature anomaly was 4.5 °C (past the elevated cutoff). With ERC at the 85th percentile (47) in NC02 Mid Coast To Mendocino, the ATLAS (SOUTHERN/CENTRAL LNU COMPLEX) ran 4 miles and grew 14,040 acres that fire day (50,009 acres across the footprint).

New large fires

Growth isn't the only question: offshore and dry days also start the fires that become large. On the 691 offshore season days past the elevated cutoff, a fire that went on to burn at least 1,000 acres started in Bay Area, North Bay and Sacramento Valley foothills (Diablo / North wind) on 16 of them (2.3% of those days, against 1.3% on a typical offshore season day); about ×2.1 the usual odds for the date. That's 53% of the 30 large-fire starts there since 2001.
