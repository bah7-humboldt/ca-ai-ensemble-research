What it measuresHI

The sea-level pressure at 25°N minus 19°N along 157°W, the highest of the fire day's 2 a.m. and 2 p.m. (12 and 00 UTC) values: how hard the subtropical high to the north is pushing air toward the islands.

Why it matters for fireHI

A big north-south difference means strong, gusty trades, and the strongest cases bring damaging downslope winds to the leeward slopes. August 8, 2023 on Maui was a strong high to the north with Hurricane Dora passing far to the south: an unusually steep gradient over very dry grass.

Hawaii has no Predictive Service Areas, so the islands are grouped as Kauai, Oahu, Maui Nui (Maui, Molokai, Lanai) and Hawaii Island. A large-fire day is a day when satellite-mapped growth, or the final size of the fires found that day, reached 300 acres. Hawaii's grass fires mostly burn out within a day or two, often between satellite passes, so the fire records (FPA FOD, InFORM) carry most of the history.
